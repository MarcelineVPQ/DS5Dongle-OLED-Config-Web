# DS5 Dongle OLED Edition Backlog

**The one list** of open and planned work for the app and its firmware (`~/projects/DS5Dongle`
points here too), started 2026-10-11. Each row says what it is, where it stands, and what it is
waiting on. Rows with a detail doc link to it.

**How it's laid out:** **Fix first** (what to fix before any new feature), then four sections:
**For 0.9.5 stable** (only what is due before the stable release), **Next (0.9.6)** (a short,
ordered list), **Later**, **Ideas**; then **Shipped**.

**Keeping it current:**

- When work starts, ships, or is decided against, change its row in the same commit. A shipped
  item moves to the **Shipped** table with a **Done** status that names the build it is in (or
  "unreleased" until a build has it). It stays there while About → Coming soon lists it under "In
  the next build" (`tests/unit/roadmapBacklog.test.ts` maps every entry to a row and checks its
  status). **At each stable release the Shipped table is emptied:** its rows leave together with
  their "In the next build" entries once the release has them (the CHANGELOG lists them), and
  their detail docs (if any) move to `docs/complete/`.
- **Where a new row goes:** a bug, wrong behaviour or something explicitly agreed for the
  release under way goes into **For 0.9.5 stable** (and into **Fix first** if it is a fix);
  anything else into **Later** or **Ideas**. **Next** stays short (about 10–15) and in order. At a
  stable release, **Next** becomes the new release's section and **Fix first** is redone.
- Something agreed in conversation gets a row the same day.
- Session notes and handoffs point here. They never keep a second list.
- **The app shows a public copy of this list** (About → Coming soon, `src/desktop/about/roadmap.ts`).
  When a user-facing row is added, changes status, ships or is dropped, update its entry there in
  the same commit (plain, short, no dates; never internal details, hosts, names, telemetry or the
  easter egg). "In the next build" entries leave it once they're in a stable release.
- **Both ways:** every row still to do (any status but Done / Fixed / Superseded) has a Coming
  soon entry, unless it's internal: then its Item cell ends with **(internal)** (tooling, tests,
  hosting, naming, marketing, other people's bugs, anything a user never sees). An internal row
  has no entry, whichever section it is in. `tests/unit/roadmapBacklog.test.ts` checks both
  directions.

**Status words:** _Agreed_ (decided, not started) · _In progress_ · _Blocked_ (waiting on
something outside the project) · _Question_ (needs an answer from Marcy before anything happens) ·
_Idea_ (noted, not decided) · _Open_ (a known problem, not yet scheduled) · _Done_ (shipped: in a
Development build, or merged for the next one) · _Superseded_ (replaced by later work).

## Fix first

What to fix before any new feature starts, ranked by user impact × how many players it reaches
× effort (2026-10-13). Evidence: **verified** (measured or seen on hardware), **built** (the fix
exists on a branch, not merged or released), **code** (follows from reading the code, not yet
measured), **suspected** (a likely cause, not reproduced), **reported** (a report, not yet
checked). Every row is in [For 0.9.5 stable](#for-095-stable). No new feature starts until 1–10
are done or explicitly parked.

1. **Linux: controller volume and mic gain restored low.** _Done, unreleased (branches `fix-first-app`, then `volume-persist`: the first fix didn't stick, the levels came back low after every flash)._ Verified on the owner's machine: the
   headset mic at gain 0 (near silent) and the amp at −20 dB after every boot, replug and firmware update, for any Linux
   player whose saved ALSA state is low; the mic looks broken. Small app fix with Undo; now also saved (pkexec) and kept while the app runs.
2. **Headset: both ears on Linux's default sound profile.** Verified (right ear only), fix built
   (`dd20982`, firmware `headset-stereo`). Every Linux headset user; merge and release.
3. **Firmware: headphone volume reaches full.** Code (100 of 127, 79 % at most, every OS); fix
   built (`b263ce9`, firmware `mic-level`). Every headset user; merge, then a hardware check.
4. **Firmware: noise cancellation stays on after a headset plug or unplug.** Suspected from the
   code and upstream #263: after unplugging a headset on Linux the speaker may play at about half
   level until the controller reconnects. Quick check by ear, small firmware fix.
5. **Launchers: Heroic crashes when the app closes it.** _Done, unreleased (branch `launch-claim-095`):
   not the close itself; Heroic inherited the app's AppImage library path and crashed at exit once
   that copy of the app had gone. Launchers now get no AppImage path; hardware check before stable._
6. **Linux: stereo headset profile follows the jack.** _Done, unreleased (branch `headset-095`); hardware check before stable._ Verified (WirePlumber keeps the mono
   profile with a headset in). Every Linux headset player gets mono without it; medium effort.
7. **"Is the launcher installed?" check on every OS.** _Done, unreleased (branch `fix-first-app`)._ Code: always "installed" on Windows and
   macOS, so the app offers launcher links that can't work. One-line API swap.
8. **Windows: second launch slow, portable leftovers.** _Done, unreleased (branch `windows-installer`): a per-user installer replaces the portable exe; second start 18–21 s → under 0.6 s, nothing left in Temp._
9. **Games: verify ▶ Play for Heroic and Lutris games.** The dev.9 fix is not verified; Lutris
   Play was never tried. Play is the main button of the Games area; a test, then fixes.
10. **Release gate: hardware checks before 0.9.5 stable.** Shipped but untested on hardware: Matt's
    Edge (issue #15), a DualSense V2, PC sleep, the upstream settings batch, Microphone level and
    Echo cancellation; Windows only ever ran in a VM.
11. **Narrator line drops pieces.** Fix built (firmware branch `connect-line`): waits for a ready
    path, primes it with silence, sends at the controller's rate. Owner test: 5 power-cycles.
12. **Firmware: mic buffer that doesn't grow.** Code: the mic delay creeps from 30 to 80 ms and
    stays there; voice chat latency. Small to medium.
13. **Firmware: speaker buffer minimum 28–32.** _Built (firmware `fw-speaker-095`, app `headset-095`), waits for the hardware check._ Reported upstream (#252): below 28 the speaker
    garbles, and we allow 16. Tiny fix.
14. **Firmware: idle Bluetooth scan stops.** Reported upstream (#158/#162/#196): the board scans
    forever while idle, grabs other pads and phones, uses radio and heat. Small, needs a policy.
15. **Firmware: speaker plays both channels.** _Built (firmware `fw-speaker-095`), waits for the hardware check._ Code: the speaker plays the right channel only, so
    on Windows and macOS sound panned left is lost. Small.
16. **Test: audio and haptics after sleep/wake.** Never run: PadSense-CE's bridge
    dies after the second sleep/wake (its issue #4), and our PC sleep options shipped in dev.9.
17. **Guide: Windows controller sound set to Stereo.** _Done, unreleased (branch `headset-095`); the VM check (T5) waits for the pre-stable check._ Code: Windows' 4-channel default can put a
    game's rear channels into the haptics (buzzing grips); check the default in the VM. Guide text.
18. **Windows: Accent color "Desktop".** _Done: works since dev.10, verified in the Win11 VM
    (2026-10-14), live changes too._
19. **Windows: running-game check costs ~5.7 % CPU in the tray.** _Done, unreleased (branch
    `windows-stable`): 6.7 % → 0.08 % of the VM in the tray (native process snapshot)._
20. **{{device}} busy: "Unable to claim interface".** _Done, unreleased (branch `launch-claim-095`):
    a plain notice naming the holder where it can (Linux), Retry, and it connects by itself once
    the board is free; hardware check before stable._

Not on the list: the connect-time mic concealment ⚠ (fixed in dev.9 on the app side; the
firmware still counts it, harmless), and the colour picker (done in dev.5; the owner decides on
it at the end of the cycle, its row is in For 0.9.5 stable).
