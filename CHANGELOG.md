# Changelog

All notable changes to the **DS5 Dongle OLED Edition Configurator** (the desktop app; the retired web tool up to 0.8.0) are documented here.

Format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); versions follow [SemVer](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- **The OLED is the centerpiece of the Pico 2 W page again.** The page opens on the DS5 Dongle OLED Edition's screen, named as what it is (Waveshare Pico-OLED-1.3 · 1.3″ · 128 × 64 · SH1107), mirrored live and large, with what it's doing now beside it (brightness, on / dimmed / off, which of the 11 screens, redraws per second, frame) and the module's specs. They had ended up at the bottom of Diagnostics; they're in one place now, the OLED's, and the mirror's line under the picture is gone (it said the same). Below it in a narrow window. The dials and Diagnostics follow. All 7 languages.

### Added

- **Collapse all and Clear pins.** A small ⋮ at the top of every settings panel, the same on every page: Collapse all folds every section, pinned ones too (they stay pinned and open again next time); Clear pins unpins this page's sections. Works with the keyboard, in every language.
- **Lock the rail collapsed.** A small lock in the rail's bottom corner keeps the rail small, icons only, so it no longer opens when you point at it; click it again to unlock. Remembered across restarts.
- **Share your OLED.** A Share button beside the OLED's name on the Pico 2 W page saves exactly what the screen shows: **Save PNG** (1×, 4× or 8×, every OLED pixel a crisp square, with or without the board around it as the mirror draws it), **Copy** (the picture on the clipboard, ready to paste into Discord) and **Record GIF** (up to 10 seconds, a frame each time the picture changes, with a recording dot, Stop and Cancel). Files are saved where you pick in the system's save dialog; nothing is uploaded. It uses the frames the mirror already reads, and makes the file only when you ask, off the page's thread. Works with the simulated board too. All 7 languages.
- **KEY0 and KEY1 on the screen, like on the module.** Two keys beside the live screen, where they sit on the Waveshare board, level with the `>` and `<` the screen draws: click KEY0 for the next screen and KEY1 for the previous one; hold KEY1 for 1.5 s (a bar fills while you hold) to step the brightness, as on the board. Each key's tooltip says what it does on the screen shown; Space or Enter work the same from the keyboard. The press goes to your board (firmware with protocol 1.18), which treats it exactly like a finger on the key, the OLED wired or not; older firmware shows the keys with what to update. The simulated board (dev builds) moves through its screens the same way. All 7 languages.
- **Pick any Proton version for a game (Linux).** A game's page, Compatibility, lists every Proton and Wine build its launcher can run it with: Steam's compatibility tools (compatibilitytools.d and Valve's own Protons), Heroic's Wine and Proton builds, and Lutris's Wine versions, with the DualSense Proton build at the top as recommended and the one the game uses now marked. Picking another says what changes and which file, a running Steam, Heroic or Lutris is named as closed and reopened before anything is written, the old setting is backed up first, and Undo puts it back right there (or from the change history). Not shown on Windows or macOS, where games don't run through Proton. All 7 languages.
- **The controller buttons the screen uses, beside it.** R1, the directional buttons and △ ○ ✕ □ (Sony's glyphs) next to the live screen, so every OLED screen can be worked from the app: Settings (up / down pick, left / right change, △ saves; hold △ 2 s on Reset to defaults or Wipe all slots), Slots (pick, △ use, hold □ 1.5 s to forget), Lightbar (R1 mode, △ ○ ✕ □ save a favorite), Trigger Test (△ next effect) and Diagnostics (scroll). Click to press; hold to hold (a bar fills over the hold the screen needs). Each button's tooltip says what it does on the screen shown, and buttons a screen doesn't use are greyed out. Only the board's screen sees these presses: games never do (firmware with protocol 1.19; older firmware shows them with what to update). Settings, Slots and Diagnostics work without a controller; Lightbar and Trigger Test need one. Space or Enter work from the keyboard. The simulated board (dev builds) answers them on its Settings, Slots, Lightbar, Trigger Test and Diagnostics screens. All 7 languages.

## [0.9.5-dev.12] - 2026-10-14

### Performance

The app is meant to sip resources, not drink them. Measured before and after this release's changes, in a test copy of the app:

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linux, hidden in the tray (animated light bar) | 4.9 % CPU | 0.4 % |
| Idle window, game page | 60 frames/s | 9 |
| Idle window, About / Games Home | 60 frames/s | 10 / 10 |
| Idle window, Controller page (controller lying still) | 60 frames/s | 10 |
| Idle window, App settings | 60 frames/s | 11 |
| Window in the background (not focused) | 60 frames/s | still (0) |
| Game art memory (Controller page with the art background) | 50 MB | 11 MB |

### Added

- **Every notice can be dismissed with a small × in its corner.** An update or a problem with your board stays hidden after you dismiss it, also after a restart, until something changes (a newer version, a different problem); an error comes back if the same thing fails again. The rail keeps a yellow dot on the page where something is still waiting (an app update on App settings, a firmware update or a board problem on the board's page (Pico 2 W)) until it's done, with what's waiting in its tooltip. App settings → Dismissed notices lists what you dismissed that still applies, with a way to it and Show again. All 7 languages.
- **Developer: firmware updates and first-time setup without hardware** (dev builds, Developer card, with "Simulate the Pico 2 W" on). The simulated board now restarts into BOOTSEL like a real one and takes the firmware over the same path: the firmware card's Update runs start to finish (progress, waiting for the board, done) and it comes back reporting the version it was sent, kept across restarts. **Simulated board** puts it in BOOTSEL, makes it a blank Pico for the setup screen's first install, or takes it off USB; **Firmware update failure** makes the next update stop with a write error at 10, 50 or 90 %, never show up in BOOTSEL, or never come back; **Writing speed** picks instant, a few seconds, or a real board's pace. All 7 languages.
- **Developer: "Fake games"** (dev builds, Developer card): Games shows a made-up library of 20 games across Steam, Heroic (GOG, Epic, Amazon, sideloaded) and Lutris, with generated art, every DualSense support tier, setup that needs attention, HDR in each state, a running game and play history, so the Games pages can be worked on without launchers, a board or a controller. Play, Apply, Undo and Install change only the made-up library: no launcher file is read or written and nothing is started. Turning it off brings back the real scan. All 7 languages.
- **Short notifications at the bottom right.** One kind of message for the whole app, for what just happened: "All changes saved" after Save, "Copied" for the HDR lines, the factory reset done, a game's profile going on or off by itself, and a controller connecting or disconnecting. Each closes by itself after a few seconds (longer for longer text), waits while you point at it or tab to it, and has a × to close; screen readers read it out without interrupting. At most three show at once, the same message never twice, and nothing piles up while the app is in the tray. When the narrator's caption already says it, the notification stays away. Errors and things you need to act on still stay at the top of the page until you close them.

### Changed

- **Diagnostics and the other readouts are always shown.** Pico diagnostics, Controller info, a game's Details and Games → Home's This computer are no longer folding cards: each is a card in the page's main view, like the page's other cards, always open (a deep link still scrolls to it and flashes it once). On the Pico 2 W page, Diagnostics (the timings and their graphs, the audio counters, the rates, the OLED) stays live, updated every second while the page is shown; reads stop when you leave the page or the window is hidden. Timing's Reset stays.
- **The OLED mirror repaints only when the picture changes.** It is still read 10 times a second while shown, but an unchanged frame (including the firmware's periodic resend) no longer redraws the canvas, and Diagnostics' Redraws now counts real changes only.
- **The settings panel is a little narrower** (375 px instead of 390 px on every page), leaving more room for the main view.
- **Diagnostics' tables read as one compact block.** The timings, the audio counters, the OLED's facts and a game's Details put each value right beside its label (the label column as wide as its longest label, the sparklines after), instead of labels at the far left and values at the far right.
- **Settings rows fit any panel width.** When a row's control doesn't fit beside its label (the Developer section's selects, a narrow window's drawer), the label and its hint go on top and the control under them at full width, instead of squeezing the label into one word per line under an overlapping dropdown. Selects beside their label are all one width.
- **Long explanations are behind an ⓘ.** A small ⓘ next to a label shows the longer explanation as a tooltip on hover, or when you Tab to it (Esc closes it); screen readers read it as the label's description. What you must know before acting (a range like "0 = never", a warning like "Restarts the page.") stays on screen. All 7 languages.
- **Every rail item shows or hides its page's settings panel when you're already on that page**, as the Controller item did: Pico 2 W, Games, App settings and About too, with the same tooltip. Each page remembers its panel shown or hidden (a page you haven't visited shows it); on a narrow window the drawer follows the same state.

- **About opens faster.** App changes and Firmware changes open on their newest releases, with Show older releases under them (a chapter in the list further down shows them too); App changes now draws about 100 elements instead of 900. Each text is read once while the app runs; before, opening or folding any section read the shown text again (about 100 ms each time for App changes). All 7 languages.
- **Every page's settings fold open in place, in one panel on the right.** The **Controller** page keeps your controller on the left; the panel, titled with your controller (for example **Cosmic Red DualSense**), has **Light bar**, **Adaptive triggers**, **Haptic feedback**, **Speaker**, **Microphone**, **Button assignments**, **Sticks & calibration** and **Pairing slots**; **Controller info** stays under the drawing, folded until you open it (a yellow dot on it when Sony has newer firmware). Clicking a part of the drawing opens its section (a button: **Button assignments** at its row) instead of a separate panel. Pins are remembered per board and pairing slot. The **Pico 2 W** page keeps its console and dials on the left, with **Firmware**, **Restart & reset**, **Power & sleep**, **OLED screen**, and **USB & status pin** on the right; **Diagnostics** (graphs, audio counters, timings with Reset, specs) is under the dials, folded until you open it, and its extra readings run only while it's open. The rail's dot opens **Firmware** for a firmware update and **Diagnostics** for a board problem, as does the tray's Diagnostics. The panel is a little wider on every page. **App settings** shows your controller on the left and its sections on the right (Language, Appearance, Accessibility, Startup and window, Graphics, Updates, Privacy, Dismissed notices, Developer): click a section's name to open it and the one open before folds; the small dot in its top-right corner pins it open. Each name has a short line that says how it's set (for example **Hardware acceleration on · HDR lighting off**), and a yellow dot where something waits, so the rail's dot for an app update opens **Updates** directly. The app remembers what was open and pinned. **Advanced** is now **Graphics**. **About** shows its text on the left and its sections on the right (Guide, App changes, Firmware changes, Proton changes, Coming soon, Credits), each listing its chapters to jump to. **Games → Home** keeps your library on the left (Continue playing, the grid) with **This computer** (controller audio and access, HDR) above it, folded until you open it, and game setup on the right: **Launchers** (with Rescan), **DualSense Proton build**, **Recommended changes** (the rail's count is its yellow dot, and clicking the count opens it, also from a game's page) and **Change history**; the old "Everything is set up" line and the attention card are gone. **A game's page** keeps its art, Play bar and quick links on the left, with tiles that say the game at a glance (DualSense support, Runs with, Controller profile, Setup) and open their section; on the right are **Controller profile** (what it changes, the button layout and stick curves, **Edit in controller view**; the copy of every controller setting under "All settings" is gone, the controller view edits them), **Setup**, **HDR** (now its own section, with **Supports HDR**), **Compatibility**, **DualSense support** with your rating and **Notes**, with **Details** (store, launcher, ID, folder) under the tiles. The panel holds only what you set or do; what the app shows you (diagnostics, controller info, this computer, a game's details) is on the left. Every game's page shares its pins and keeps the game's own colors. In a narrow window the panel slides in from the right. The keyboard moves between section names with the arrow keys, Home and End. A closed section draws and checks nothing: a folded Speaker or Microphone stops its meter, a folded Pairing slots its slot list. All 7 languages.
- **The app draws almost nothing while you're not using it.** The green "Connected" dot pulses three times when the controller connects, then stays steady (it pulsed forever, which kept the Controller page redrawing at the screen's refresh rate). A controller lying still no longer redraws the page: the motion sensors' tiny resting jitter doesn't count as a change, and the Connection numbers (updates a second, round trip) change once a second. The background's crossfade between games takes 4 s in small steps with the slow pan instead of 2.5 s at full speed. While another window (or a game) is in front of the app, the background holds completely still. Measured on a test copy at 1920 x 1080 with a resting controller, frames a second: Controller 55 → 10, App settings 41 → 11, Games home 22 → 10, Pico 2 W 28 → 20 (its screen mirror is live), game page and About 9 to 10 (the pan); in another window's shadow, 0.
- **Windows: the app is now installed, instead of running as a portable exe.** Download `…-win-x64-setup.exe` and run it: it installs for your Windows user only (no admin rights needed), adds the app to the Start menu and an uninstaller (Settings → Apps), and opens it. The portable exe unpacked about 1 GB to Temp on every start: it took about 10 seconds to open, 18–21 seconds to show its window when started again from the tray (and that deleted the running copy's tray icons and bundled firmware), and left copies behind. Installed, the app opens in about a second and shows its window again in under one. Updates download the new installer, check it against the signed feed and install it quietly when you press **Restart now** (or when you quit). **Using the portable exe? It can't update itself to this:** download the installer once; your settings and game profiles carry over, the app says once that the old `.exe` file can be deleted, and it tidies what the portable exe left in Temp. Uninstalling removes the app, its shortcuts, launch at login and any waiting update; your settings stay.
- **Linux: the running-game check costs less while the app sits in the tray.** It looked at every one of your processes every 2 seconds. It now reads only a short line per process, reads a process's details once instead of on every look, and while the window is in the tray and no game has a profile it looks every 10 seconds (with a profile, or with the window open, still every 2 seconds, so a game's profile switches as quickly as before). Measured on a test copy in the tray for 5 minutes, % of a processor core: 0.40 → 0.33 with a game profile, 0.40 → 0.11 without.
- **The swirl background draws 10 frames a second instead of 12.5**, the same as the game-art background's pan, at the same speed. Measured on a test copy: about 20 % less graphics work on every page it shows on, with no visible difference.
- **A game's rating now says what it rates: your DualSense in that game, not the game.** Five stars under a game's banner read as a rating of the game. The rating moved into **DualSense support**, under the per-feature list, and asks **How well does your DualSense work in this game?** Each star says what it means when you point at it or move to it with the keyboard (**Doesn't work**, **Barely works**, **Works with problems**, **Works well**, **Works perfectly**), and the one you pick stays written next to the stars. The arrow keys, Home and End pick a star. **What did you notice?** and **Saved on this PC** are kept, and ratings you already gave stay as they are. All 7 languages.
- **Games uses less memory for game art.** The app loaded every picture at its full size (Steam heroes are 1920 px wide, Heroic and Lutris art often larger) even where it is shown small: the library's 20 px icons, the cover grid, the logos and the Controller page's blurred background. Each place now gets a copy scaled to the size it shows, sharp on 1x to 2x screens, made once and kept in the app's settings folder (at most 128 MB, oldest removed first; deleted when you turn off Games' access to your launchers). In a test library of 40 games the decoded image memory dropped by about half on Games and by about 75% for the Controller page's art background.
- **Newer Electron (45, a beta for now).** The app runs on Electron 45 (Chromium 156). On KDE Plasma (Wayland), clicking the tray icon now brings the window to the front, and a minimized window comes back, where before the taskbar entry only flashed.

### Fixed

- **Warning text in section headers is readable in every theme and colorway.** A section's yellow status (a newer firmware on the board's page, a task waiting) used a fixed amber that fell to 1.5–4.4:1 on panels in some controller colors (Sterling Silver, Starlight Blue, Fortnite, custom bodies); it now uses the warning color the theme system makes legible on every panel. Screen readers also no longer find the side panel's sections while the panel is hidden (narrow windows).
- **Popups open on top again, everywhere.** The language menu (App settings → Language) opened under the sections after it and was cut off since the settings moved into sections. Every popup now opens above everything else, wherever it is (a section, the narrow window's drawer, a card): the language menu, the color picker, an ⓘ's explanation, Games' Show menu and the Developer channel dialog. A section no longer cuts off anything inside it once it has unfolded.
- **macOS: the menu-bar icon follows a light or dark menu bar.** It is now a template image, which macOS tints: a filled dot when the controller is connected, a hollow ring when the board has no controller, no dot and a faded controller without a board, and the charging bolt cut out of the controller. Linux and Windows keep their colored icon.
- **macOS: no error lines at every start.** Launch at login is only changed when it differs from the setting (unregistering a login item that was never registered logged an error), and the Controller page no longer asks for game art where the Games page doesn't exist.
- **Firmware updates and first-time setup** (found by running them against the simulated board):
  - The first install's progress bar on the setup screen never showed; it now fills as the firmware is written.
  - After a finished update, the board leaving USB again (its controller turning off, or unplugging it) brought back "Updating firmware, don't unplug" and hid the setup screen. A finished update now stays finished.
  - Right after the first install, the setup screen showed "A Pico is ready to set up" again for a moment while the Pico restarted. It now goes straight to pairing your controller.
  - A file that isn't firmware for the Pico 2 W (made for another chip, or cut off) is refused in plain words before anything is written; the board keeps its firmware. "Didn't show up in BOOTSEL mode" is now in all 7 languages.
- **A game's Play or launcher link that couldn't be opened said so for only 6 seconds.** The error now stays until you dismiss it or try again (Games → Home and the game page).
- **The app kept the graphics card busy while open and untouched.** The background's slow pan and zoom was an endless animation, so the window was redrawn at the screen's refresh rate (60 to 240 times a second) on every page, and with Glass or see-through cards each redraw blurred the background again. The pan now takes 10 small steps a second (4 when another window is in front, none while a game runs and the app isn't in front), along the same path at the same speed; the swirls take their pan step with their own frames. The live controller view stops redrawing when nothing it shows has changed (a controller put down that sends the same values), and App settings is no longer rebuilt with every controller reading. Measured on a test copy at 1920 x 1080 with a resting controller, frames a second: About 60 → 10, a game page 60 → 9, Games home 60 → 22, Pico 2 W page 60 → 28 (its screen mirror is live), App settings rebuilds −30 % CPU; the Controller page stays busy because of the pulsing "Connected" dot and the live sensor readouts (left as they are).
- **The app used about 2.6 % of a processor core while hidden in the tray** (6 hours overnight with the board and a controller connected). With an animated lightbar (rainbow, breathing, a charging pulse) the controller's color changed on every one-second check, and each change redrew the hidden window, redrew the tray icon and rebuilt the tray menu. While the window is hidden, a new lightbar color now reaches the tray icon once it holds for a second, and a color that keeps changing updates it at most once a minute; anything else, such as a slot switch, charging or the controller disconnecting, still shows at once, and nothing changes while the window is open. The tray icon is drawn without the graphics card, the tray menu is rebuilt only when what it shows changes, and the running-game check (for game profiles) checks each process's owner once instead of every 2 seconds. Measured hidden for 5 minutes with a simulated board: animated lightbar 4.9 % → 0.4 % of a core, steady lightbar 0.38 % → 0.32 %.
- **Windows: the running-game check cost about 6.7 % of a 2-core PC while the app sat in the tray** (Games on). Every 3 seconds the app started Windows' `tasklist` to see which programs run, and starting it cost more than the check. The app now reads the list of running programs itself, in about a millisecond (`tasklist` stays as a fallback). Measured in the tray for 5 minutes on a 2-core Windows 11 PC: the app 3.9 % plus tasklist ~2.7 % → 0.08 %. Game profiles switch as before.
- **Windows: copies of the app piled up in the Temp folder.** The portable exe unpacks about 1 GB to Temp on every start and deletes it when the app quits; when that was cut short (the launcher stopped, or Windows signing out or shutting down with the app in the tray) the copy stayed, about 1 GB each time (24.6 GB on our test PC). The app now removes its own old copies 20 seconds after it starts, and never the one it runs from.

## [0.9.5-dev.10] - 2026-10-13

### Fixed

- **An update feed that failed its security check read as "Are you online?".** When the update feed's signature didn't check out (the development feed's signature was stale for hours), every place that checks for updates said you might be offline: App settings' **Check for updates**, the setup screen, the firmware card (**can't check (offline)**) and the Development channel dialog, while Games → Proton install showed the raw English error. The app now tells the failures apart: no network still asks about the connection; a feed that fails its signature check says **The update feed didn't pass its security check, so nothing was installed. This is on our side, not your connection: try again later.**; a feed it can't read says so the same way; the Development channel dialog says when no development build is published yet, and the Proton install shows the same plain messages (the raw error goes to the log). All 7 languages.
- **Windows: a launcher the app started kept part of the app open after it closed.** A Steam the app reopened (after a Games change) or started for ▶ Play inherited the app's open handles, including a network socket it was listening on, and held them after the app quit. Launchers are now started through Windows itself (like a double-click), so they get none of the app's handles. Linux and macOS are unchanged.
- **Windows: the Games tooltip in the navigation rail mentioned Proton setup**, which exists on Linux only. On Windows it now reads "your games and per-game profiles". All 7 languages.
- **A new controller couldn't pair with all four pairing slots taken, and nothing said why.** With every slot full the board stops taking new controllers, so one doing Create + PS just blinked until it gave up. Pairing slots now says **All 4 slots are taken. To pair a new controller, forget one first.** (from the board's real slots; the Developer card's pretend controllers never count), and with the matching firmware the OLED's Slots screen says **All full: forget one**. The Guide's Pairing slots section says so too. All 7 languages.
- **Accent color → Desktop kept the theme's own accent (Light theme: green instead of KDE's pink).** The desktop's accent was only read for the **Match desktop** theme; with Light, Dark or Controller, choosing **Desktop** just dropped the controller's color. It now applies the desktop's accent in every theme (buttons, selection, highlights; the theme's surfaces stay as they are). The app also found KDE Plasma only through XDG_CURRENT_DESKTOP, which is missing when the app is started by `systemd-run`, some autostart entries or Flatpak/AppImage launchers; it now also checks KDE's own session variables, and a kdeglobals file with Plasma or KWin running. The accent is read from KDE's kdeglobals first, then the desktop portal, then the system. If none can be read, the Accent color row says **Couldn't read your desktop's accent color — using the app's**. All 7 languages.
- **A firmware update that failed partway kept the board claimed, so trying again could fail too.** When writing the firmware stopped (a cable or USB hiccup), the app never let go of the board's update connection, so it stayed busy until you unplugged it, and the error was a raw USB message. The connection is now always released, also when the update fails, so installing again works right away; the message says in plain words that the update stopped, that the board is waiting in BOOTSEL mode, and that the app offers to install the firmware again (or to unplug it and plug it back in if nothing shows up). All 7 languages.
- **Failures that showed nothing now say what happened and what to do.** An error pass went through every error path in the app and the firmware, each now shown in plain words (all 7 languages) or logged on purpose. Fixed:
  - Apply and Undo on Games → Home: an error showed for a moment, then the rescan right after wiped it. A request the app's own check refuses now says so in plain words, not as a raw error.
  - A setting that can't be saved (App settings, the tray's toggles, the narrator, "Keep the app up to date?", Games consent) is put back, and the app says so. Games consent no longer changes the window before the setting is saved.
  - A game's page: creating or deleting a profile, profile edits, rating, tags and notes that fail to save; saving, updating or deleting a button layout.
  - A game's profile that can't go on before the game starts (its normal settings stay on), and normal settings that can't be put back after the game.
  - Download and install for an app update that fails: the banner used to disappear; it now says why until dismissed.
  - Controller info → Refresh with Sony unreachable now says so. Undo now says when restarting the audio service failed. The tray's slot switch, and links and launcher links that don't open, now say so.
  - The mic test and the Windows USB repair no longer get stuck on an unexpected error.
  - Your board's answers read in plain words ("couldn't finish that, for example saving to its memory") instead of "Command 0x04 failed". With firmware from this pass, a slot switch, forget or wipe, or Edge extra-button change whose save to the board's memory fails now says so, where it used to look saved.
- **With "Check for updates automatically" off, the app still went online for updates.** It looked up the firmware feed at every start, on the setup screen, after switching the update channel and when the Development channel dialog opened. Now, with the switch off, the app makes no update request at all (the update feed, the firmware on offer, the Development channel preview, Sony's controller firmware list) until you press **Check for updates** (or a check button that says it goes online, like the Development channel dialog's **Check the development feed**). The switch says so, and the firmware card shows **not checked (automatic checks are off)** instead of a version. The Games page's check for our newest DualSense Proton follows the Games permission, which names it. All 7 languages.
- **A firmware update could install an older build than the one it offered.** The offer came from a fresh look at the update feed, but Install downloaded from the feed of the last update check, up to 6 hours old, or the other channel's after switching. Install now downloads exactly the version you were shown and confirmed, checks the file against that version's checksum, and if the feed has changed since, installs nothing and says **A newer firmware appeared. Check again.** One update check also reads the feed once instead of two or three times. All 7 languages.
- **Games → Home: a game's big Continue playing tile showed a flat colored panel instead of its art** (Ghostrunner from GOG via Heroic: a plain red tile, though its cover showed in the grid and the list). The tile's own background color reset how the art is sized and placed, so the picture showed at full size from its top-left corner: for Ghostrunner's 2560-pixel-wide GOG background, a slice of flat red. The art now fills the tile like the game page's header. Both use one lookup: the game's hero art, else its wide capsule, else its cover shown whole on the right of a gradient in the art's color, else the gradient alone (also under any picture that won't load). A game page without hero art now shows its cover the same way instead of a blank header.
- **Linux: the controller's volume and microphone level fix didn't stick.** After **Turn the controller's volume and microphone level up to full**, they were low again after the next firmware update, replug or restart, and the change came back on Games → Home. Each time the controller's sound card appears, Linux sets it to the levels it last saved, and the fix only changed the current ones. Apply now also saves them as Linux's saved levels (one password prompt, the system's own; the fix says so first), so they stay full; while the app runs it also sets them back to full whenever the controller reconnects and says **Controller volume restored to full**. ## [0.8.0] — 2026-10-10

The first desktop release: the configurator is now an app, released together with the firmware it includes. **Tested on Linux; the Windows and macOS builds are included but untested.**

### Added

- **Desktop app (Electron)**, built from the same codebase as the web tool. Tested on Linux; Windows and macOS builds are included but haven't been tested yet. Settings apply to the dongle live; "Save to dongle" persists them, "Revert" restores the saved state.
- **WebUSB transport** to the firmware's new configurator interface (firmware v0.8+, `ENABLE_COMPANION_VENDOR`). Configuration works again from the browser too: Config and Remap were disabled since firmware v0.6.12.
- Slot switch / forget / wipe, dongle restart, BOOTSEL, factory reset and trigger presets from the app, with confirmed results.
- Live audio-pipeline counters (the `0xF5` diagnostics) and controller signal quality.
- Self-hosted fonts (no Google Fonts request).
- **System tray.** Close-to-tray (default), launch at login, start minimized. The tray icon is the DualSense with its touchpad lit in the controller's actual lightbar color, a charging bolt, and a status dot (green connected, amber no controller, grey no dongle); the tooltip shows battery. The menu has slot switching, diagnostics and update actions.
- **App updates.** The app checks a `latest.json` feed (20 s after launch, then every 6 h; opt-out in ⚙ App settings or the tray), downloads with progress, verifies size and SHA-256 (fails closed), installs and restarts. **Install updates automatically** (asked once on first run, and in ⚙ App settings) downloads, verifies and installs them in the background; the new version starts the next time you open the app. Older versions are never installed. Linux AppImages keep a stable link so menu entries survive updates; the Windows portable exe swaps itself in place.
- **One-click firmware updates.** Downloaded and verified by the app, then the dongle reboots into BOOTSEL on command and is flashed over USB. No drive mounting, no unplugging; the version is confirmed when it reconnects.
- **Packaging.** Linux AppImage that adds itself to the application menu on first run; Windows portable exe that adds a Start Menu shortcut; macOS zip. App icon: the DualSense on a green tile (Zacksly art, CC BY 3.0).
- **Linux USB setup.** A one-time "Set up USB access" button installs a udev rule (via pkexec) for users without Steam's rules.
- **About page.** The app's CHANGELOG ("What's new"), the firmware CHANGELOG (fetched from GitHub), the README as a guide, and credits/licenses, rendered in the app without raw HTML or remote images. After an update, a one-time banner links to the new notes.
- **"Match desktop" theme.** A fourth theme option that follows the desktop's colors: KDE Plasma's full color scheme (backgrounds, text, accent) read from `kdeglobals` and updated live when it changes; the system accent color on Windows, macOS and portal-aware Linux desktops. Text contrast is enforced (WCAG AA or better), and status colors stay fixed.
- **Games page (Linux).** Checks Steam, Heroic (Epic, GOG, Amazon, sideloaded) and Lutris for settings that keep adaptive triggers and haptics from reaching games: the wrong Proton, Steam Input forced on, `PROTON_PREFER_SDL` on a DualSense game, a Proton that no longer exists, the controller's audio on a pro-audio profile, and missing hidraw access. It reads nothing until you allow it on a first-visit screen that lists exactly what it checks ("Stop checking" turns it off again). Each suggested change says what it does, why, and which files it writes. Nothing happens until you review the changes and press Apply. Running launchers are closed only on request (cleanly, never while a game runs). Every file is backed up first, and Undo restores it byte for byte. Undo refuses rather than overwrite settings a launcher saved after the change. The page is fully translated. It also installs and updates the DualSense Proton build from a SHA-256-verified download, and the Games button shows how many changes are recommended.
- **Studio credits.** About → Credits opens with the 42 Pixel Studios card: the dice mark, "Developed by Marcy Kuhn", and links to the studio site, GitHub, the source and issue reporting.
- **Speaker level up to +12 dB** (firmware v0.8+), centered on 0 in the app.
- **One screen, built around your live controller.** The DualSense is drawn in the middle and moves as you use it: buttons light up, L2/R2 fill as you pull them, sticks move, touches show on the touchpad, the lightbar and player indicator glow like the real thing. Click any part to set it up in the side panel (lightbar, trigger effects, remapping per button, speaker, haptics, mic, pairing slots). The dongle, Games, About and the app's own settings open from the top bar. Art: AL2009man's DualSense (MIT).
- **Your controller, in its own colors.** The app recognizes the connected controller (DualSense or DualSense Edge) and its colorway (Cosmic Red, Midnight Black, Starlight Blue, special editions ...) and draws it to match. Edge back paddles and Fn buttons show live.
- **Tray icon follows the taskbar:** dark on a light taskbar, light on a dark one.
- **Releases come from the firmware repo** ([DS5Dongle-OLED-Edition](https://github.com/MarcelineVPQ/DS5Dongle-OLED-Edition/releases)): one release per version with the app for every OS and the firmware it was built with (ADR-0005). The update feed lives there too.
- **The app includes its firmware.** It installs it on a new Pico (plug it in holding BOOTSEL: one click, no download, no files to copy, works offline), and offers it to dongles with older firmware. A newer published firmware is offered instead when there is one.
- **First-time setup screen.** With no dongle connected, the app explains how to connect one, finds a Pico in BOOTSEL mode, installs the firmware and walks you through pairing. The Controller page shows how to pair while no controller is connected.

### Security
